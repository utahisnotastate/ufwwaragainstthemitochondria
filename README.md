# UFW: War Against the Mitochondria

## The Seven Open Millennium Prize Problems for Biology

> Life is not only chemistry. It is also shape, electricity, water, and time.
> These seven puzzles are still open. Below, each one is written so a child could teach it back.

**Status key**

| Mark | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Solved | Scientists agree enough to teach it in school / textbooks |
| Partly solved | Real progress, but the full answer is still missing |
| Still open | We do not yet have a complete, honest answer |
| Not true | A flashy claim that fails careful science (kept out of our “solved” column) |

**Quick scoreboard**

| # | Problem (kid name) | Solved? | One-sentence status |
|---|--------------------|---------|---------------------|
| 1 | How did life turn on? | Still open | We have clues (pores, wet–dry cycles), not a finished recipe |
| 2 | How does a body get its shape? | Partly solved | Genes matter; electric patterns in tissue also help steer shape |
| 3 | How do proteins fold so fast? | Partly solved | They relax like bubbles; water helps; we still refine the physics |
| 4 | How does the mind hold together? | Still open | Brain electricity is well mapped; “one unified feeling” is not fully explained |
| 5 | Why do we age? | Partly solved | Many causes known; no single “off switch” proven |
| 6 | What is the “quiet” DNA for? | Partly solved | Much is regulation & structure; not all roles are mapped |
| 7 | Why do germs win sometimes? | Partly solved | Germs are real; a sick “terrain” also matters; both, not either/or |

### Problem 1 — How did life turn on? *(Abiogenesis)*

**Explain it to a friend:** Imagine shaking a box of Legos forever and hoping a robot builds itself. That almost never works. Something in the *room* (shapes, wet–dry cycles, surfaces) must help pieces line up. We still do not know the first full “turn on” story of life on Earth.

| | |
|--|--|
| **Kid question** | How did non-living stuff become living stuff? |
| **Solved** | Living cells need energy, membranes, and information copies. Chemistry alone is not the whole story. |
| **Partly solved** | Mineral pores, wet–dry cycles, and surface chemistry can help molecules organize. |
| **Still open** | The exact first pathway from chemistry → metabolism → heredity on Earth. |
| **Not true** | Instant “life machines” from magic water batteries or vacuum impedance gadgets. |
| **More** | [Kid page](./academic/millennium_resolutions.md#1-how-did-life-turn-on) · [Science notes](./academic/formalisms/01_interfacial_water.md) |

### Problem 2 — How does a body get its shape? *(Morphogenesis)*

**Explain it to a friend:** Sprinkle sand on a drum and play a note—the sand jumps into a pattern. Change the note, change the pattern. Your cells are a bit like the sand. DNA is important, but it is not a finished 3D drawing of your hand. Electric patterns across tissues also help tell cells where to go.

| | |
|--|--|
| **Kid question** | Why is a hand shaped like a hand and not like a kidney? |
| **Solved** | Genes and proteins build cell parts. Embryos follow known developmental steps. |
| **Partly solved** | Tissue voltage patterns and cell–cell electrical links help guide anatomy (active research). |
| **Still open** | A full “map of voltages → finished organs” you can read like a blueprint for every animal. |
| **Not true** | “Just shine the right electric hologram and a damaged organ rebuilds in hours” as proven medicine. |
| **More** | [Kid page](./academic/millennium_resolutions.md#2-how-does-a-body-get-its-shape) · [Science notes](./academic/formalisms/02_bioelectric_morphogenesis.md) |

### Problem 3 — How do proteins fold so fast? *(Levinthal’s paradox)*

**Explain it to a friend:** A soap bubble does not do homework to become a sphere. It relaxes into the comfy shape. Proteins are long chains that fold into tools. They do not try every possible twist; they fall into a good shape, and water around them helps.

| | |
|--|--|
| **Kid question** | How can a protein find the right shape so quickly? |
| **Solved** | Folding is physical relaxation, not a computer guessing every angle. Many structures are known. |
| **Partly solved** | Funneled energy landscapes, chaperones, and hydration physics; prediction tools keep improving. |
| **Still open** | Perfect real-time folding physics inside a crowded living cell (not only in a crystal or a model). |
| **Not true** | Zap amyloids with a special laser and they always snap healthy in milliseconds (not proven medicine). |
| **More** | [Kid page](./academic/millennium_resolutions.md#3-how-do-proteins-fold-so-fast) · [Science notes](./academic/formalisms/03_protein_folding_thz.md) |

### Problem 4 — How does the mind hold together? *(Neural code & binding)*

**Explain it to a friend:** A radio does not keep the whole song inside the battery. It tunes waves. Your brain’s sparks and chemistry are real and well studied. How all those parts become *one* feeling, and how memory lasts, is still a deep puzzle. Comparing the brain only to a hard drive is too small a story—but “memories live in outer space” is not a school answer either.

| | |
|--|--|
| **Kid question** | Where do thoughts and memories live? |
| **Solved** | Nerve signals use ion channels (Hodgkin–Huxley style). Many brain maps and drugs are based on this. |
| **Partly solved** | Other ideas (mechanical waves in membranes, weak light from cells) are studied carefully and debated. |
| **Still open** | How scattered activity becomes one conscious scene; how memory is stored at every scale. |
| **Not true** | A home gadget that “retunes” you to a cosmic memory radio. |
| **More** | [Kid page](./academic/millennium_resolutions.md#4-how-does-the-mind-hold-together) · [Science notes](./academic/formalisms/04_nerve_soliton_models.md) |

### Problem 5 — Why do we age? *(Senescence)*

**Explain it to a friend:** Play Telephone: whisper a secret down a line and it gets messy. Aging is a bit like many tiny copying and repair mistakes stacking up. There is no single chalkboard magic room that erases all aging. Healthy habits help; immortality machines do not.

| | |
|--|--|
| **Kid question** | Why do bodies wear out over time? |
| **Solved** | Many aging “hallmarks” are known (mitochondria stress, protein clutter, telomere change, inflammation, and more). |
| **Partly solved** | How these causes talk to each other; which levers help healthspan most in humans. |
| **Still open** | A complete, safe way to stop or reverse aging as a whole. |
| **Not true** | Faraday cages + Earth-hum tones that reset your mitochondria and telomeres on demand. |
| **More** | [Kid page](./academic/millennium_resolutions.md#5-why-do-we-age) · [Science notes](./academic/formalisms/05_aging_mitochondria.md) |

### Problem 6 — What is the quiet DNA for? *(Dark / noncoding genome)*

**Explain it to a friend:** In a book, the letters matter—but so do spaces, margins, and the index. Most DNA does not print proteins. A lot of it helps decide *when* and *where* genes fire. Calling all of it “junk” was too rude. Calling it secret psychic software is too wild.

| | |
|--|--|
| **Kid question** | Why is most DNA not a protein recipe? |
| **Solved** | Much noncoding DNA is regulation, structure, and evolutionary leftovers—not random trash. |
| **Partly solved** | Huge catalogs of enhancers and chromatin states; math models of DNA opening (PBD). |
| **Still open** | A complete job list for every quiet stretch of the genome. |
| **Not true** | Play a special beep and awaken hidden superpowers written in “junk.” |
| **More** | [Kid page](./academic/millennium_resolutions.md#6-what-is-the-quiet-dna-for) · [Science notes](./academic/formalisms/06_noncoding_dna_pbd.md) |

### Problem 7 — Why do germs win sometimes? *(Terrain & pathogenesis)*

**Explain it to a friend:** Flies love spilled food, not a clean table. Germs are real. A sick, messy body terrain also makes infection easier. Wipe the floor *and* respect germs—do not pretend flies invent the trash, and do not pretend trash invents the flies.

| | |
|--|--|
| **Kid question** | Why do the same germs hurt one person and spare another? |
| **Solved** | Many diseases are caused by microbes; vaccines and antimicrobials save lives. Barriers and immunity matter. |
| **Partly solved** | How host oxygen, pH, voltage, and immunity change who gets sick; mechanical ideas about virus shells (lab research). |
| **Still open** | Perfect prediction of every infection outcome from “terrain + germ” alone. |
| **Not true** | Shake viruses apart with a pocket speaker and “voltage clamp” them into friendly pets (not medicine). |
| **More** | [Kid page](./academic/millennium_resolutions.md#7-why-do-germs-win-sometimes) · [Science notes](./academic/formalisms/07_pathogen_mechanics.md) |
