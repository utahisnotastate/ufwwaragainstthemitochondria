# 6. Noncoding DNA & Peyrard–Bishop–Dauxois Models

**Evidence tier:** Noncoding genome has regulatory / structural roles — **A**. PBD lattice models of base-pair opening — **A–B**. SAW “intron un-silencing” that activates latent telepathy / regeneration modules — **X**.

## Scientific question

How do mechanical and thermal fluctuations open DNA base pairs, and what do noncoding regions actually do?

## Accepted baseline (Tier A)

- Most of the human genome does not encode proteins; much of it is involved in regulation, chromatin architecture, repeats, and lineage history. Calling it uniformly “junk” is outdated; calling it an “OS root directory of psychic modules” is fiction.
- Transcription is controlled by promoters, enhancers, chromatin state (e.g., histone marks), and 3D genome folding — not by consumer RF transducers.

## PBD model (Tier A–B)

The Peyrard–Bishop–Dauxois Hamiltonian is a standard nonlinear lattice model for DNA denaturation / bubble formation ([Phys. Rev. Lett. 62, 2755](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevlett.62.2755); [Phys. Rev. E 47, 684](https://doi.org/10.1103/physreve.47.684)):

\[
H_\mathrm{PBD}
=
\sum_n \left[
\frac{p_n^2}{2m}
+ V_\mathrm{Morse}(y_n)
+ W_\mathrm{stack}(y_n,y_{n-1})
\right]
\]

with Morse hydrogen-bond potential

\[
V_\mathrm{Morse}(y_n)=D_n\bigl(e^{-a_n y_n}-1\bigr)^2
\]

This is useful for melting curves and bubble dynamics in silico / theory. It does **not** authorize mapping Zipf–Mandelbrot “textual entropy” of introns to a clinical acoustic eigenvalue that opens heterochromatin on demand.
