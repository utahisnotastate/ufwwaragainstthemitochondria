# Seven Open Millennium Problems for Biology

## (Written so a child can explain them)

This page matches the big tables on the [main README](../README.md).  
Here each problem gets a short story, then a clear **Solved / Partly solved / Still open / Not true** table.

**Remember:** A good metaphor helps you *learn*. It does not invent a magic machine.

---

## 1. How did life turn on?

### Story you can tell
Imagine a thousand Legos in a box. Shake forever. A robot almost never appears.  
Now imagine the box has grooves that guide the bricks. Then patterns form faster.  
Earth’s first life probably needed helpful “grooves” too—surfaces, wet then dry cycles, tiny pores—not only random soup.

### Status table

| Column | Plain answer |
|--------|--------------|
| **Kid question** | How did dead stuff become living stuff? |
| **Solved** | Life needs energy flow, a boundary (like a bag), and a way to copy information. Pure “shake chemicals forever” is a weak story. |
| **Partly solved** | Lab and geology ideas (pores, wet–dry, surfaces) can help molecules line up. |
| **Still open** | The true first path life took on Earth. |
| **Not true** | Instant life from special “battery water” gadgets or vacuum-matching machines. |
| **Deeper notes** | [01_interfacial_water.md](./formalisms/01_interfacial_water.md) |

## 2. How does a body get its shape?

### Story you can tell
Sand on a drum jumps into pretty shapes when you play a note. Change the note → change the picture.  
Your cells are a bit like the sand. DNA helps build the tools, but tissue electricity also helps say “hand here, not kidney here.”

### Status table

| Column | Plain answer |
|--------|--------------|
| **Kid question** | Why do organs have the shapes they have? |
| **Solved** | Genes and proteins matter. Embryos follow known growth steps. |
| **Partly solved** | Voltage patterns across cells help steer growth and repair in model animals. |
| **Still open** | A complete readable “voltage map → finished body” for every organ and species. |
| **Not true** | Proven clinics that “project a shape hologram” and regrow organs in hours. |
| **Deeper notes** | [02_bioelectric_morphogenesis.md](./formalisms/02_bioelectric_morphogenesis.md) |

## 3. How do proteins fold so fast?

### Story you can tell
A soap bubble does not calculate every atom. It relaxes into a sphere.  
A protein is a long chain that folds into a tool. It does not try every twist like a lost tourist. It falls into a good valley—and the water around it is part of the room, not empty air.

### Status table

| Column | Plain answer |
|--------|--------------|
| **Kid question** | How does a protein find its working shape so quickly? |
| **Solved** | Folding is physics (relaxing), not endless guessing. Many protein shapes are known. |
| **Partly solved** | Energy “funnels,” helper proteins (chaperones), and better computer predictors. |
| **Still open** | Perfect live-cell folding physics for every hard case (including disease clumps). |
| **Not true** | A laser protocol that always un-clumps sick proteins in milliseconds as medicine. |
| **Deeper notes** | [03_protein_folding_thz.md](./formalisms/03_protein_folding_thz.md) |

## 4. How does the mind hold together?

### Story you can tell
Smash a radio and you will not find tiny musicians inside. The song is a pattern you tune.  
Your brain’s sparks are real. Doctors use that knowledge every day.  
Still: how many sparks become *one* “you,” and how memory lasts, is not finished homework.  
“Memory lives in outer space” is a fun story—not a classroom fact.

### Status table

| Column | Plain answer |
|--------|--------------|
| **Kid question** | Where do thoughts and memories live? |
| **Solved** | Nerve electricity via ion channels is well understood and medically useful. |
| **Partly solved** | Extra ideas (membrane waves, weak cell light) are researched and argued about. |
| **Still open** | Binding (one scene from many parts) and full memory storage rules. |
| **Not true** | Home “mind tuners” that download memories from a cosmic field. |
| **Deeper notes** | [04_nerve_soliton_models.md](./formalisms/04_nerve_soliton_models.md) |

## 5. Why do we age?

### Story you can tell
Play Telephone. The message gets fuzzy down the line.  
Aging is a bit like many tiny fuzzy copies and tired repair crews.  
Looking at a clear chalkboard (good repair, good habits) helps. A secret Faraday room that rewinds you like a movie is not real medicine.

### Status table

| Column | Plain answer |
|--------|--------------|
| **Kid question** | Why do we get older and weaker? |
| **Solved** | Many aging causes are listed (mitochondria stress, junk proteins, telomeres, inflammation…). |
| **Partly solved** | Which causes matter most together; what safely helps people live healthier longer. |
| **Still open** | Stopping or fully reversing aging as one clean fix. |
| **Not true** | Earth-hum chambers that cancel aging noise and reset cellular clocks. |
| **Deeper notes** | [05_aging_mitochondria.md](./formalisms/05_aging_mitochondria.md) |

## 6. What is the quiet DNA for?

### Story you can tell
Erase all the spaces in a book. The letters smash together. Hard to read!  
Most DNA is not a protein recipe. A lot of it is more like spaces, margins, and stage directions: *when* and *where* to turn genes on.  
Not junk. Also not a hidden spellbook of superpowers.

### Status table

| Column | Plain answer |
|--------|--------------|
| **Kid question** | Why is most DNA not a protein recipe? |
| **Solved** | Much quiet DNA helps control genes and genome structure. |
| **Partly solved** | Huge maps of switches; math models of DNA opening and closing. |
| **Still open** | A full job description for every quiet stretch. |
| **Not true** | Beep a speaker and “turn on” latent psychic modules in junk DNA. |
| **Deeper notes** | [06_noncoding_dna_pbd.md](./formalisms/06_noncoding_dna_pbd.md) |

## 7. Why do germs win sometimes?

### Story you can tell
Flies show up for spilled food, not for a clean table.  
Germs are real. A messy, tired body also makes infection easier.  
Clean the spill *and* respect the germs. Do not only swat flies while ignoring the trash—and do not pretend trash invents flies.

### Status table

| Column | Plain answer |
|--------|--------------|
| **Kid question** | Why do the same germs hurt one kid and skip another? |
| **Solved** | Many illnesses are caused by microbes; vaccines and antimicrobials save lives. Immunity and barriers matter. |
| **Partly solved** | How host condition changes risk; lab ideas about shaking virus shells with sound. |
| **Still open** | Perfect “terrain + germ” forecasts for every person and every bug. |
| **Not true** | Pocket devices that shatter viruses and make pathogens friendly pets. |
| **Deeper notes** | [07_pathogen_mechanics.md](./formalisms/07_pathogen_mechanics.md) |

**Don’t smash the radio looking for the song.**  
Study genes *and* shapes *and* electricity *and* water.  
Keep wonder. Keep honesty. Leave the fake gadgets on the shelf.
